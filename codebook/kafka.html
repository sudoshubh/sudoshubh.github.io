01

The whole system, in one frame

Producers write to partitions. Partitions live on brokers. Consumer groups read independently — and that independence is the whole reason Kafka isn't a queue.

remember

Kafka is a log with bookmarks. The broker stores the log; each group stores only its bookmark.

Anatomy of a partition

Four positions live on every partition, and interviewers ask about all four. Learn them as regions on one strip.

remember

Green is done, blue is your lag, orange is not yet safe. Consumers never see orange.

03

How a record picks its partition

Four branches, checked in order. Most people only know the middle one.

the trap

The modulo is over the current partition count. Grow 6 → 12 and user‑42 lands somewhere new while its old records stay put. Per-key ordering is broken from that moment on, permanently.

The three timeouts, on one clock

Two threads, two failure modes. Once you see the lanes, the configs stop being interchangeable names.

remember

Session = is there a pulse. Poll interval = is there progress. A consumer can have a perfect pulse and still get evicted for making none.

09

The rebalance storm

The most common Kafka incident in production, and a favourite "walk me through debugging this" question. It's a loop that feeds itself.

10

The producer send path

send() does not send. It appends to a buffer and returns. A background thread does the sending — which is why batching configs matter so much.

How the idempotent producer works

Where duplicates and losses come from

Two orderings of the same two operations. There is no third option without an external transaction — so the real question is which failure you'd rather design around.

Delete vs compact

Two cleanup policies. One is a stream with an expiry date, the other is a table.

The night-before sheet

If you only reread one thing, make it this. These are the answers that sound right and are wrong.

They askThe tempting answerWhat to say instead
Does Kafka guarantee ordering?YesPer partition only — and only while the partition count is stable and the producer is idempotent
Can I add consumers to go faster?Yes, scale the deploymentOnly up to the partition count. Past that they're assigned nothing
How do I preserve ordering with retries?max.in.flight=1Outdated. With idempotence on, 5 in flight is safe and much faster
How do I fix a hot partition?Add partitionsDoesn't help — one hot key still hashes to one partition. Use a composite key
What does acks=all wait for?All replicasAll in-sync replicas. With a shrunken ISR that can mean one
Does exactly-once fix duplicates?YesOnly Kafka-to-Kafka. External sinks need their own idempotency
Why isn't retention deleting my data?It should beThe active segment is never deleted; retention works on closed segments
Does max.poll.records limit the fetch?YesNo — that's max.partition.fetch.bytes. One is network, one is buffer handoff
How do I retry a failed record?Sleep and retrySleeping blocks the poll loop into a rebalance. Use retry topics
Where do consumer offsets live?On the consumerIn __consumer_offsets on the brokers
Should I give the broker a big heap?YesNo — Kafka leans on the OS page cache. 6–8 GB heap, lots of RAM
Does a new group read from the start?YesOnly with auto.offset.reset=earliest. The default is latest, so it skips everything
closing line 1

Partitions are the unit of parallelism, so consumer scale-out is capped at partition count — and partition count is capped by what your brokers and rebalance times can absorb.

closing line 2

The poll loop is single-threaded for group management, so anything that blocks processing longer than max.poll.interval.ms gets the consumer evicted. That's the root cause of most rebalance storms.

closing line 3

I default to at-least-once with idempotent consumers. Duplicates are cheaper to design around than the throughput and complexity cost of transactions — and exactly-once doesn't extend past Kafka anyway.
